Apache stdcxx

stdcxx is the Apache C++ Standard Library: Rogue Wave's standard library implementation, open-sourced in 2005, developed through 2008, dormant after, and retired to the Apache attic in 2012. It is a complete C++98 library with its own iostreams, locales and containers, built by a configuration system that characterizes the compiler with a few hundred small test programs rather than trusting version numbers. I was one of its committers.

The story begins at Hewlett-Packard, where Alexander Stepanov and Meng Lee developed the STL, with contributions from David Musser. HP made the code freely available, and Rogue Wave took that work forward as part of its broader commercial C++ Standard Library. It was less a wholesale handover than a seed that grew into a larger product. The people involved were already working together: Stroustrup recalls Tom Keffer and Nathan Myers from Rogue Wave joining an STL implementers' meeting at HP in 1994.

The relationship continued commercially, with HP licensing Rogue Wave's standard library for its C++ compiler products by 1996. Rogue Wave developed and shipped the library for years before contributing its implementation to the Apache Software Foundation in July 2005. That contribution became Apache stdcxx—the next chapter in a lineage that connected HP's original STL work, Rogue Wave's commercial development, and Apache's open-source stewardship.

The revival builds the last maintenance line against a current GCC and glibc, twenty years of drift in the toolchain and none in the tree. The rule is to work in the library's own idiom: where a platform has moved, write a characterization for what it now offers and let the headers branch on the answer, never pin the compiler back or patch around the machinery. Each failure gets the same treatment, and the ones worth writing up become notes.
